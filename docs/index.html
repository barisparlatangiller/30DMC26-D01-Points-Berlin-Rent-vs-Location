Does a “good address” cost more?

Berlin's rent index labels every address as a simple, medium or good location. We scored 360,000 addresses and every building against what tenants actually pay in their 100 m block.

3 · The scoreboard

Each address gets a label level (simple 1 · medium 2 · good 3) and a rent level (its block's rent in Berlin's cheapest, middle or most expensive third). Score = rent level − label level. Click a square to show only those places.

        Planning areas (LOR) with at least 50 addresses with rent, by average score.

        Method. Every address of the Berliner Mietspiegel 2026 location map is placed in its Zensus 2022 100 m grid cell (Gitter-ID) and receives that cell's average net rent (Nettokaltmiete, €/m²). Rent levels are Berlin-wide thirds of address-level rent. Each LoD2 building takes the majority label of the addresses inside it (or within 10 m) and the rent of the cell at its centre.

        Limits. Zensus rent describes existing tenancies on 15 May 2022, not today's asking rents, and is suppressed in cells with few rented flats; values in brackets are less reliable. The label describes the wider surroundings, not a single flat. This is a spatial comparison of area-level data, not a statement about individual flats, landlords or households.
